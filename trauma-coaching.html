TRAUMA-INFORMED COACHING

When the past keeps affecting the present.

Difficult experiences do not always stay neatly in the past. They can influence how we respond to pressure, relationships, conflict, uncertainty and even the way we see ourselves.

Trauma-informed coaching creates a calm space to slow things down, understand what is happening now and begin separating present-day choices from patterns shaped by what has already happened.

The aim is not to force disclosure or repeatedly revisit painful experiences. We work with where you are today, what you need, what you can influence and what a steadier way forward could look like.

UNDERSTANDING THE IMPACT

Sometimes the reaction makes sense when you understand the history.

Difficult or overwhelming experiences can shape the way we anticipate danger, interpret other people's behaviour, respond to conflict and protect ourselves emotionally.

What once helped somebody cope can later become exhausting. Hypervigilance, shutting down, overthinking, people pleasing, difficulty trusting, strong reactions to conflict or constantly preparing for the worst can all leave a person feeling as though life is being lived in permanent defence.

Coaching provides room to notice those patterns without shame, understand what may be keeping them active and identify where greater choice can be created in the present.

COACHING & THERAPY

Knowing when specialist support is needed matters.

Trauma-informed coaching is not psychotherapy and does not diagnose or treat PTSD or other mental-health conditions. Where somebody needs trauma treatment, crisis support or specialist mental-health care, an appropriately qualified clinical professional or NHS service is the right place for that work.

Coaching can instead focus on present-day understanding, personal agency, boundaries, decision-making and practical movement forward.

How Can The Grey Principles Help Me Build a Positive Mindset When I Am Going Through Absolute Hell?

Coaching

When you are going through absolute hell, being told to ‘think positively’ can feel dismissive or even insulting.

You cannot smile your way out of grief, meditate away betrayal, or cover genuine pain with inspirational quotes. A positive mindset does not mean pretending that everything is fine. It means learning how to stop your darkest moment from convincing you that your entire future will be dark.

That is where The Grey Method begins.

You Do Not Have to Choose Between Positivity and Pain

Life is rarely entirely light or entirely dark. Most of us live somewhere between the two: in the grey.

The Grey Method does not ask you to deny what has happened, excuse the people who hurt you, or force yourself to feel grateful while your world is falling apart. It helps you face reality without allowing that reality to consume your identity.

You can acknowledge:

  • ‘This is hurting me.’

  • ‘I do not know how this will end.’

  • ‘I am angry, frightened, or exhausted.’

  • ‘I am struggling today.’

You can still add:

  • ‘But this moment is not the whole of my life.’

  • ‘I can decide what I do next.’

  • ‘I do not have to abandon myself.’

  • ‘There may still be a way forward.’

That is genuine positivity. It is not denial. It is psychological balance.

Finding the Still Centre

When life becomes overwhelming, the mind often moves towards extremes:

‘Everything is ruined.’

‘I will never recover.’

‘Nothing will ever change.’

These thoughts can feel like facts because intense emotions narrow our attention. The Grey Method helps you slow this process and return to what I call the still centre: the space between what has happened to you and how you choose to respond.

You may not be able to control another person, reverse the past, or immediately change your circumstances. However, you can begin reclaiming control over your attention, boundaries, decisions, and next actions.

Sometimes, the first positive step is not feeling happy. It is simply becoming steady enough to breathe, think clearly, and avoid making today’s pain worse.

Separating the Situation from Your Identity

When suffering continues for a long time, it can begin to define you. You stop thinking, ‘I am experiencing trauma,’ and begin believing, ‘I am broken.’

The Grey Method challenges that connection.

You are not your worst experience.

You are not the rejection, betrayal, accusation, mistake, or loss you have endured. These things may form part of your story, but they do not have the authority to write its ending.

Coaching can help you identify the difference between:

  • What happened and what you now believe it means

  • What you can influence and what you must release

  • A painful feeling and a permanent truth

  • Protecting yourself and emotionally imprisoning yourself

  • Accepting reality and approving of what happened

That separation creates breathing room, and breathing room creates choice.

Positivity Through Action, Not Pretence

The Grey Method treats a positive mindset as something you practise, rather than something you either possess or lack.

During difficult periods, progress may look like:

  • Getting out of bed and completing one necessary task

  • Saying no without repeatedly explaining yourself

  • Interrupting a cycle of rumination

  • Choosing not to react to deliberate provocation

  • Rebuilding a routine after chaos

  • Recognising when your mind is predicting events rather than reporting facts

  • Taking one action that supports the person you want to become

These steps may appear small, but they send an important message to the mind: ‘I still have some influence over my life.’

Positive thinking becomes much more believable when positive action supports it.

You Do Not Need to Be Fearless

The purpose of coaching is not to transform you into someone who never feels fear, sadness, or anger. Those emotions carry information. The aim is to help you experience them without automatically surrendering control to them.

  • Courage is not the absence of fear. It is the decision that fear will not make every choice for you.
  • The Grey Method helps you ask:
  • ‘What is this emotion trying to tell me?’
  • ‘Is my response protecting me or keeping me trapped?’
  • ‘What would the calmest, clearest version of me do next?’
  • ‘What is within my control today?’
  • These questions shift the mind from helplessness towards personal agency.

When You Cannot See the Whole Path

When you are in hell, you do not need to see the entire road out. You only need to identify the next safe and meaningful step.

That might mean establishing a boundary, challenging an unhelpful belief, creating a daily structure, asking for appropriate support, or learning how to regulate yourself before responding.

You do not have to heal everything at once.

The Grey Method Coaching meets you where you are, not where other people think you should be. It helps you work with the reality of your circumstances while gradually rebuilding clarity, self trust, and hope.

It is also important to understand that coaching is not a replacement for medical treatment, psychotherapy, or emergency support. Where deeper clinical support is needed, responsible coaching should work alongside it. It should not attempt to replace it.

The Grey Is Where Your Power Returns

A positive mindset does not mean saying, ‘Nothing bad is happening.’

It means being able to say:

‘This is one of the hardest periods of my life, but I am still here.’

‘I may not control everything happening around me, but I can work on what happens within me.’

‘I can feel pain without becoming pain.’

‘I can lose parts of my old life without losing the possibility of creating something new.’

The Grey Method is not about escaping into false light or remaining trapped in darkness. It is about finding the steady ground between them: the place where truth, acceptance, and purposeful action can exist together.

You might be going through absolute hell.

But hell is a place you are moving through.

It does not have to become your permanent address.

Begin Walking the Grey

If you feel trapped between who you were and who you are becoming, The Grey Method Coaching can help you find your centre, rebuild your mindset, and take your next steps with greater clarity.

You do not need to pretend that everything is positive.

You only need to believe that another step is possible and be willing to take it.
